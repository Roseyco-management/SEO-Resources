# Search & AI Research Workflow for SEO

How to use Perplexity, Google, and AI search tools for market research, topic discovery, competitor analysis, and content planning.

---

## Why This Matters

Before you write anything or optimize anything, you need to know:
- What your audience is actually searching for
- What questions they have
- What your competitors are doing
- What gaps exist that you can fill
- What AI tools are saying about your space

Perplexity and Google are your primary research tools. This guide shows you exactly how to use them.

---

## Part 1: Market Research with Perplexity

Perplexity is the fastest way to understand a market because it synthesizes multiple sources and cites them — giving you both answers and source material.

### Understand Your Market

```
Prompts to use in Perplexity:

"What are the biggest challenges facing [industry] in 2026?"
"What are the top trends in [industry] right now?"
"What do customers complain about most with [type of product/service]?"
"What are the most common questions people ask about [topic]?"
"What is the current market size for [industry/niche]?"
"Who are the market leaders in [niche] and what do they do differently?"
```

**What to do with the results:**
- Save the cited sources — these are potential link building targets and content inspiration
- Note the specific language and terms used — these become your keywords
- Identify recurring pain points — these become your content topics

### Find Content Gaps

```
"What topics about [subject] are poorly covered online?"
"What are the most common misconceptions about [topic]?"
"What questions about [topic] don't have good answers online?"
"Compare the content strategies of [competitor 1] vs [competitor 2]"
"What subtopics of [main topic] are underserved?"
```

**What to do with the results:**
- Content gaps = low competition opportunities
- Misconceptions = great blog post topics ("myth vs reality" posts rank well)
- Unanswered questions = FAQ pages and featured snippet opportunities

### Competitor Research

```
"What does [competitor] do well for SEO?"
"What content does [competitor website] publish most frequently?"
"What are customers saying about [competitor] in reviews?"
"What features does [competitor] offer that others don't?"
"How does [your industry] compare pricing between providers?"
```

**What to do with the results:**
- Map competitor content to find what you're missing
- Find negative reviews of competitors — address those pain points in your own content
- Understand pricing landscape for your pricing page

### Audience Research

```
"What does a typical [job title/persona] care about most?"
"What tools does a [persona] use daily?"
"What communities and forums do [target audience] hang out in?"
"What podcasts and blogs do [target audience] follow?"
"What are the buying criteria for [product category]?"
```

**What to do with the results:**
- Communities/forums = where to promote content and find keyword ideas
- Buying criteria = what to emphasize on your sales pages
- Tools they use = integration opportunities and comparison content

## Part 2: Google Search for SEO Research

Google itself is one of the best keyword research tools. It tells you exactly what people search for.

### Google Autocomplete Mining

Type your main keyword into Google and DON'T hit enter. Write down every suggestion.

```
[your keyword] a...
[your keyword] b...
[your keyword] c...
... through z

[your keyword] how...
[your keyword] what...
[your keyword] why...
[your keyword] when...
[your keyword] best...
[your keyword] vs...
[your keyword] for...
[your keyword] near...
```

**Each suggestion is a real search query people use.** Collect them all — they become your keyword list.

### "People Also Ask" Mining

1. Search your main keyword on Google
2. Click on the "People Also Ask" questions
3. Each click reveals MORE questions
4. Keep clicking — you can generate 50+ questions this way
5. Each question = a potential content piece or FAQ entry

### "Related Searches" at Bottom

Scroll to the bottom of any Google search results page. The "Related searches" section shows you:
- Variations of your keyword
- Related topics Google associates with your search
- Long-tail keyword opportunities

### SERP Analysis (What's Already Ranking)

For any keyword you want to target, search it on Google and study the top 10 results:

| What to check | Why it matters |
|--------------|----------------|
| What type of content ranks? (blog, product page, video, listicle) | Create the same type |
| How long are the top articles? | Match or exceed the depth |
| What subtopics do they all cover? | These are must-include sections |
| What's missing from all of them? | This is your competitive edge |
| Are there featured snippets? | Structure your content to win them |
| Are there videos ranking? | Consider creating video content |
| Are there "People Also Ask" boxes? | Answer those questions in your content |

### Google Search Operators for Research

```
site:competitor.com                    → See all indexed pages
site:competitor.com/blog               → See their blog content
site:competitor.com intitle:"keyword"  → Find their pages targeting a keyword
"keyword" -site:yoursite.com           → See who else ranks for your keyword
allintitle:"keyword phrase"            → How many pages target this exact phrase
inurl:keyword                          → Pages with keyword in URL
```

## Part 3: Using Perplexity for Topic Clustering

This is where it gets powerful. Use Perplexity to build your content plan.

### Generate a Topical Map

```
"If I wanted to be the #1 authority on [topic], what subtopics would I need to cover?
List them organized as pillar topics and supporting cluster topics."
```

```
"Create a comprehensive topic map for a website about [niche].
Organize as main categories → subtopics → specific article ideas."
```

### Validate Keywords

```
"Is [keyword] a high-intent commercial keyword or informational?"
"What is the search intent behind [keyword]?"
"What type of content ranks best for [keyword]?"
"Is [keyword] competitive or could a new site rank for it?"
```

### Generate Content Briefs

```
"Create a content brief for an article targeting the keyword [keyword].
Include: recommended title, H2 headings, key points to cover, questions to answer,
and what the top-ranking articles cover that we should match or beat."
```

### Find Long-Tail Opportunities

```
"What long-tail keyword variations exist for [main keyword]?"
"What specific questions do beginners ask about [topic]?"
"What are niche subtopics within [broader topic] that have less competition?"
```

## Part 4: Ongoing Research Habits

### Weekly (15 minutes)

- [ ] Search your main keywords on Google — has the SERP changed?
- [ ] Check Perplexity for your brand — are you being cited?
- [ ] Search your topic on Perplexity — what sources are being cited? (those are your competitors in AI search)
- [ ] Note any new "People Also Ask" questions

### Monthly (1 hour)

- [ ] Run your main keywords through Google Autocomplete again — new suggestions = new opportunities
- [ ] Ask Perplexity: "What's new in [your industry] this month?"
- [ ] Search competitor domains: `site:competitor.com` — what new content did they publish?
- [ ] Check if any of your content is being cited by Perplexity/ChatGPT

### Quarterly (half day)

- [ ] Full SERP analysis for your top 20 keywords
- [ ] Competitor content audit — what are they publishing that you're not?
- [ ] Update your topical map with new subtopics
- [ ] Ask Perplexity to audit your content strategy: "Here's what my site covers [list topics]. What major gaps exist for a [your niche] website?"

## Part 5: Research-to-Action Cheat Sheet

| Research finding | Action to take |
|-----------------|----------------|
| Found a question with no good answer | Write a comprehensive answer post |
| Found a competitor's top-performing content | Create a better version (skyscraper technique) |
| Found a keyword with buyer intent | Create a landing page or product page |
| Found negative reviews of competitors | Address those specific pain points on your site |
| Found a community discussing your topic | Engage there + create content addressing their discussions |
| Perplexity cites your competitor but not you | Study what they have that you don't (stats, structure, authority) |
| Google shows videos for your keyword | Create video content |
| Google shows a featured snippet | Structure your content with the exact format (paragraph, list, table) |
| Found a trending topic in your space | Create timely content fast |
| Found common misconceptions | Write "myth vs fact" content |

## Part 6: Perplexity vs Google — When to Use Which

| Task | Use Google | Use Perplexity |
|------|-----------|----------------|
| Find what people search for | Yes (autocomplete, PAA) | |
| Understand search intent | Yes (look at what ranks) | |
| Quick market overview | | Yes (synthesized answer) |
| Find cited sources | | Yes (with citations) |
| Competitor page analysis | Yes (site: operator) | |
| Generate content ideas | | Yes (better at brainstorming) |
| Validate a keyword | Yes (check actual SERP) | Yes (check search intent) |
| Find content gaps | | Yes (ask directly) |
| Check if AI cites you | | Yes (search your topic) |
| See real search volume | Yes (Google Keyword Planner) | |
| Deep topic research | | Yes (multi-source synthesis) |
| Local SEO research | Yes (Google Maps, local pack) | |
